## Overall Statement

Siren is an open-sourced disco track that was mixed and mastered in Pro Tools. The original stems provided a solid foundation, but many of the components lacked clarity because of frequency overlap and masking. The main purpose of this mixing process was to enhance the overall clarity and increase the impact of the arrangement so that every element could contribute to the groove and energy typical of disco music.

## Bass Bus
In the original recording, the bass was too soft and did not provide the drive that disco music demands. Since bass is one of the most important components in this genre, I decided to divide the single bass bus into two. The first bus was processed with T-Racks 5 White 2A compressor to push the low-end presence forward, combined with EQ adjustments to remove unnecessary frequencies below 60Hz and emphasize the range around 100Hz. This gave the bass more weight and definition. The second bus focused on the higher-frequency components of the bass. By applying PSA-1 Amplifier and T-Racks 5 Comprexxor, I created a glitch-like energetic texture that layered on top of the low end, adding both power and excitement to the overall bass sound.

## Guitar Buses
The guitar section required a lot of attention because guitars play a central role in providing harmonic structure and groove. They also contained significant reverb in the raw stems, which made clarity an essential goal. For the acoustic guitars that primarily provided harmonic support, I reduced the high frequencies and panned them to the left and right, opening up the stereo image. In contrast, the strumming-focused acoustic guitars had their high frequencies retained and sometimes boosted, and they were kept in the center to highlight rhythmic clarity. Electric guitars were treated in a similar way: strumming buses had their mid and low frequencies reduced to match the clarity of the acoustic strumming, while the harmonic buses received a boost in the midrange and were compressed to cut through the mix. These adjustments allowed both acoustic and electric guitars to maintain presence without clashing with each other or other instruments.

## Synth Buses
Synth pads were another essential element in building the harmony of the track. I boosted the midrange around 1200Hz and the high frequencies around 18kHz to fill out the spectrum, and then used T-Racks 5 Quad Image to widen the stereo field, giving the pad a lush, expansive quality. At the same time, a Black 76 Compressor was applied to reduce the pad’s energy so that it did not overwhelm other parts of the mix. For the plucks, the mid and high frequencies were reduced to prevent conflicts with the pads, allowing them to sit in the mix as supportive but not intrusive elements.

## Vox Buses
The vocal stems had issues with excessive sibilance, particularly heavy “ess” sounds that negatively impacted the listening experience. I used Pro-DS to control these and reduce the harshness. The lead vocals were also slightly rolled off in the high frequencies to prevent sharpness, and further smoothed with a Black 76 Compressor to even out dynamics. The backing vocals had their low-mid frequencies reduced, which helped them avoid muddying the mix and conflicting with drums and bass.

## Drum Buses
The kick drum was shaped using the EQP-1A, which not only boosted the low-end energy but also added analog warmth and thickness. This gave the kick both impact and body. For the hi-hats, mid frequencies were reduced to make space for other instruments, while the high frequencies were emphasized to improve clarity and groove. Cymbal rolls were attenuated to control their overwhelming presence. Several electronic drum loops were also present, but they contained too much low frequency that clashed with bass and other instruments. To solve this, I removed much of the low end and boosted the mids and highs, allowing them to function more as percussive layers that supported the hi-hats and snares, enhancing rhythmic depth without crowding the low spectrum.

## Mastering
For mastering, I applied the TR5 Bus Compressor to bring all the buses together and make the track sound more consistent. After that, I used the TR5 Tape Machine with the 30ips Glue preset, which added some texture and a bit of soft clipping. This made the track sound warmer and more rounded, and it also gave the song a smoother overall feel that fits well with disco music.
